# Stray — Pitch Summary

## The One-Liner

Every map app shows you the fastest way somewhere. Stray shows you everywhere you've never been.

## The Problem

You've lived in your city for years and you've walked maybe 5% of it. The same commute, the same coffee shop loop, the same three parks. You don't even know what you're missing because you've never had a reason to look. Existing fog-of-war apps treat exploration as a game to be completed — checklists and progress bars. They turn the joy of wandering into a spreadsheet.

## What Stray Does

Stray covers your map in dark fog. When you walk somewhere, the fog dissolves and the real map blooms into color beneath it. That's it. That's the core.

But the texture is what makes it interesting:

**The fog is your compass.** You don't need Stray to tell you where to go. Just look at the map. The dark patches are the invitation. The fog itself is the guide.

**Your habits become visible.** Cells you revisit glow warmer — cool blue for a single visit, transparent for familiar ground, warm amber for daily routes, bright glow for home base. Your map becomes a living heat signature of your life. Patterns you'd never notice on your own become obvious. That street you walk every day. That neighborhood three blocks away you've somehow never entered.

**Sessions record your walks.** Tap "Start Session" and Stray traces your path on the map as a white polyline. Pause, resume, end. See your duration, distance, new cells discovered, steps. A quiet record of the walk, not a performance metric.

**Your photos live on the map.** Stray reads your photo library's geolocations (never copies them) and shows thumbnails right on the cells where they were taken. Tap a cell and see the photos you took there. Your camera roll becomes a spatial memory.

**Time travel through your history.** The timeline scrubber lets you slide through past days and watch your fog state at any point in time — see your exploration accumulate day by day, with that day's photos appearing above the scrubber.

**Tap any cell to inspect it.** Visit count, first and last visited date, city, photos taken there. Add notes. Mark places as Home, Work, Favorite, or anything custom. Your map becomes annotated with personal meaning.

**Zoom out and see the big picture.** At wide zoom levels, explored regions collapse into blue badges showing how many cells you've uncovered in each area. A bird's-eye view of everywhere you've been.

## What Stray Doesn't Do

- No turn-by-turn navigation. The fog points, it doesn't direct.
- No streaks, no leaderboards, no loss-aversion notifications. Walking is not a game to be optimized.
- No social features. This is personal. Your map is yours.
- No performance tracking. Steps are estimated for curiosity, not fitness.

## Who It's For

People who like walking and are curious about what's around the corner. People who've played Pikmin Bloom or Fog of World and wanted something less gamified. Travelers who want to deeply explore a new city instead of just hitting the tourist spots. Anyone who's looked at a map and thought "I've never been to that street."

It's not for athletes, completionists, or people who need an app to tell them what to do.

## The Philosophy

Stray is inspired by the Situationist International's concept of the *derive* — an unplanned journey through a landscape, guided by curiosity rather than efficiency. Guy Debord wrote about disrupting habitual patterns to see your environment with fresh eyes. Stray is that idea turned into software.

Anti-optimization. The fog doesn't reward you for filling it in. It just shows you the shape of your world — and how much of it you haven't seen.

## Tagline Alternatives

"Life Cartography" is generic — it could describe any mapping app. The tagline should capture the *feeling* of Stray, not just its category.

**Top candidates:**

1. **"See how far you haven't gone."** — Provocative. Challenges you. The fog is the message.

2. **"Your map has holes in it."** — Direct, slightly uncomfortable, true. Makes you want to fix it.

3. **"Walk the gaps."** — Short, imperative, captures the derive spirit. The gaps are the point.

4. **"The world you haven't walked."** — Descriptive but evocative. Implies vastness.

5. **"Every street is a question."** — Poetic. Reframes walking as curiosity, not exercise.

**Honorable mentions:**

- "Fog lifts when you show up." — Emphasizes presence, but a bit long.
- "The map remembers." — Atmospheric, hints at personal history layer.
- "Wander with intent." — Oxymoronic in a good way, but risks sounding self-helpy.
- "Not all who stray are lost." — Too close to the Tolkien quote. Cliche.
- "Unknown territory." — Clean, but generic.

**My recommendation:** **"Walk the gaps."** It's three words, it's an action, it captures the derive philosophy, and it sounds like nothing else in the App Store. It works as a tagline, a call to action, and a description of the core experience all at once.

Runner-up: **"See how far you haven't gone."** More provocative, better for marketing copy and ads, but less clean as a persistent tagline.
